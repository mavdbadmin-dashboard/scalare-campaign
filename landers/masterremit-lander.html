The $208 Trillion Market — And the $30 Transfer Fee That Created a £10 Billion Company

$208T Annual global cross-border payment flows · The world's largest financial market

The cross-border payments industry is the largest financial market most retail investors have never thought about. $208 trillion flows across borders annually — dwarfing equities, bonds, and even foreign exchange spot markets by volume. Within that, the remittance segment — money sent by migrants to their home countries — represents $800 billion per year, growing by roughly 5% annually, and structurally resistant to recession because it is driven by labour migration, not economic cycles.

The opportunity was hiding in plain sight. Banks charged migrants 5–8% per transfer. Western Union charged 4–7%. MoneyGram, similar. The actual cost of a digital transfer — once infrastructure was built — was closer to 0.3%. The gap between price and cost was not a feature. It was a legacy, sustained only because no one had built a better alternative at scale.

Wise built it. Starting in 2011 as TransferWise, the company used real mid-market exchange rates and a peer-to-peer architecture to deliver transfers at a fraction of traditional costs. By the time it listed on the London Stock Exchange in 2021, it was valued at £11 billion — one of the largest direct listings in UK market history. Remitly, focused on the US-to-emerging-market corridor, listed on Nasdaq the same year at a valuation above $7 billion.

The lesson was clear: whoever builds the infrastructure for the corridors Wise and Remitly haven't dominated wins the next chapter.
