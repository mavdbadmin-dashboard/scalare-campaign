Every year, the global fire detection industry generates more than 500 million false alarms. Emergency crews scramble. Factories go dark. Buildings empty. The economic toll runs into the tens of billions of dollars annually — lost productivity, diverted first responders, ruined equipment.

And yet the real fires — the ones that actually kill people and destroy infrastructure — are still being detected, on average, four to seven minutes too late.

That gap is not a footnote. It is the entire problem.

Traditional smoke detectors work on simple chemistry: a particle of smoke reaches a sensor, a threshold is crossed, an alarm sounds. They have no context, no intelligence, no ability to distinguish between a burning building and burned toast. The false alarm rate in commercial and industrial settings hovers above 40%. And when a real fire starts — especially in the early, oxygen-starved smoldering phase — these systems are frequently silent until it is far too late.

The global fire protection market alone is a $93.8 billion industry, on track to hit $134 billion by 2030. But fire detection is only the beginning of what Roze AI is building. Add AI-powered disaster prevention, critical infrastructure monitoring, industrial safety, power grid protection, wildfire forecasting, and smart city safety systems — and the total addressable market expands to over $340 billion by 2030. It is one of the largest, most structurally underserved technology opportunities on earth, and it is almost entirely built on technology that hasn't fundamentally changed in fifty years.

A Decade of Work, Quietly Deployed

In 2015, a South Korean engineer named Young Jin Cho founded a company called ROZETATECH in Seoul. Cho is not a startup founder in the traditional sense — he is a 30-year veteran of IT and AI development who had already built and led technology businesses across the US and Korea. When he turned his attention to fire detection, he was not interested in building a better smoke alarm. He was building something categorically different.

The company he created — now called Roze AI, Inc. — spent nearly a decade developing what it calls the DAP: the Disaster AI Platform. This is not a sensor. It is a wireless network of monitoring devices layered on top of a digital twin simulation engine — a real-time virtual representation of a physical building or facility, continuously fed by AI-analyzed sensor data. The system doesn't wait for smoke to reach a detector. It models the environment, identifies anomalies in heat, airflow, gas concentrations, electrical patterns, and structural conditions, and flags developing disasters before they become uncontrollable.

Fire is just the first application. The same platform monitors industrial gas leaks, power grid anomalies, flood risk conditions, earthquake structural responses, and chemical hazard events. FIRE 4 CAST™ — Roze AI's predictive forecasting system — applies machine learning to environmental and atmospheric data to predict where fires are likely to occur before they start. RozeAI® EnerGen™ extends this intelligence to power generation monitoring, protecting the infrastructure that modern society depends on. The platform is already operating across South Korean military bases, government facilities, power plants, and designated industrial production sites. It is not a demo. It is infrastructure.

The difference in detection time is not incremental. It is measured in minutes. Sometimes more.

Roze AI's platform has been installed and operated at major government facilities, military bases, designated industrial production sites, and power plants across South Korea. It has not been tested in a lab. It has been running, in production, at some of the most critical infrastructure in the country, for years. The Korean government evaluated it and certified DAP as an innovative new technology — a designation that comes with both validation and, more practically, procurement advantages.

The product line has expanded. So.S HOME™ is an all-in-one consumer safety device powered by sound-field sensing. FIRE 4 CAST™ is the company's AI fire forecasting system — predictive, not reactive. RozeAI® EnerGen™ is an AI system-on-chip (SoC) developed for disaster prevention and power generation monitoring. RozeAI® Adviser™ is a portable AI safety device for field deployment.

This is not a single-product story. It is a platform.
